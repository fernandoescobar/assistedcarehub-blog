Rexburg is home to BYU-Idaho, and for many small assisted living homes that shapes who applies for caregiver work. Rexburg caregiver jobs often attract people whose hours change from one semester to the next, and who may work only a few shifts a week. That can suit a small home well. It also means the paperwork and training for each person has to be done carefully and in the right order.

This article sets out what Idaho's assisted living rule requires before a new caregiver works with residents, and a practical way to run the first weeks. Idaho's rule applies statewide, so nothing here is different in Madison County than in the rest of Idaho. If your home is in another state, requirements differ, and your own state's licensing agency is the place to check.

## 1. Start the background check before the first shift

Idaho's rule (IDAPA 16.03.22, section 009) requires a criminal history and background check for employees and contractors who have direct access to residents. The Idaho Department of Health and Welfare check satisfies this requirement. If a disqualifying crime is disclosed, the person must not have direct resident access.

While results are pending, the rule allows a person to work only under another employee who already has a cleared check. That person may not have one-to-one contact with a resident, or access to a resident's belongings, without the supervision of a cleared employee.

The Department's Background Check Unit explains how applicants apply online and are fingerprinted. In practice:

- Ask every applicant to begin the application as soon as you make an offer.
- Write down the date each application was submitted and the date each clearance arrives.
- Decide in advance who the cleared supervising employee is for any person still waiting.

## 2. Plan the 16 hours of orientation

Section 625 requires staff to complete orientation specific to their job within thirty days of hire. At least sixteen hours of job-related orientation must be completed before a person provides unsupervised personal assistance to residents, and before working alone. The rule leaves the means and methods of training to the facility.

Orientation must include resident rights, the reporting and documentation requirements for allegations of abuse, neglect and exploitation, and your relevant policies and procedures. It must also be appropriate to the population you serve.

For part-time caregivers, the hours are easy to lose track of. A simple approach is to break orientation into short, named sessions, and have the trainer and the new caregiver sign and date each one. Your administrator can then see at a glance how many hours are complete and who may be scheduled alone.

## 3. Treat medication training as a separate step

Orientation does not cover medication assistance. Section 645 requires that, before assisting residents with medications, staff complete a medication assistance course offered by one of Idaho's community colleges or a curriculum approved by the Department. That training is in addition to the minimum orientation hours. Staff also need training on documentation, and on how to respond when a resident refuses or misses a medication, receives an incorrect one, or when a medication is unavailable or missing.

The facility nurse must then delegate and document assistance with medications. Each medication assistant is delegated individually, including skill demonstration, and again any time the licensed nurse changes.

A new caregiver can be valuable long before this step is complete. Many homes simply schedule such a person for tasks that do not involve medications until the course and the nurse's delegation are on file. Your nurse and licensing agency are the right people to confirm how that works in your home.

## 4. Build part-time caregiver schedules around coverage rules

Part-time hours are useful, but the staffing rules in section 600 still apply to every shift. Staff must be up and awake and immediately available during resident sleeping hours. At least one direct care staff member with first aid and CPR certification must be in the facility at all times.

When you add a part-time caregiver to the schedule, check three things:

- Is a cleared, oriented employee on the shift alongside anyone still in training?
- Is at least one person with current first aid and CPR certification on every shift, including evenings, weekends and breaks between semesters?
- Does the as-worked record (section 330.11) show direct care staff names and the shifts actually worked?

It is worth asking part-time staff early about their class schedules and exam weeks, and about when they expect to be away. A short, written availability sheet kept by the administrator costs little, and it helps prevent gaps.

## 5. Keep the personnel file complete from day one

Section 330.10 lists what each employee's record must contain. Among other items, it includes a job description, signed and dated records of all required training, copies of CPR and first aid certifications, evidence of medication training, background check results, and the licensed nurse's documentation of delegation to unlicensed staff.

Section 640 also requires each employee to receive a minimum of eight hours of job-related continued training per year. If someone works few hours, ask your licensing agency how that requirement applies to them, and keep a dated record either way. When a policy changes, section 641 requires the date of the change to be noted on the policy and staff training to be updated, so part-time staff need to be included in that training as well.

## A simple order for every new caregiver

1. Make the offer and start the background check application.
2. Confirm who supervises the person until the clearance arrives.
3. Complete and sign off the sixteen orientation hours before any unsupervised work.
4. Add first aid and CPR certification to the file, and note its expiry date.
5. Complete the medication assistance course and the nurse's delegation before the person helps with medications.
6. Calendar the annual continued training and any policy-change training.
