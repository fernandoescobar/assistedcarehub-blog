For owners and administrators of an Idaho Falls assisted living home, a medical appointment is rarely just an appointment. The clinic may be across town or an hour down the highway, the weather may change between breakfast and the return trip, and the home still has to be staffed while someone is away. A small home feels this more than most.

The same questions come up in Idaho Falls, Blackfoot, Rigby and Rexburg: who goes, who drives, what travels with the resident, and what gets written down afterwards. This article offers a simple way to plan each trip. It is general guidance, not legal advice, and each home should confirm its own obligations with its licensing agency and its attorney.

## 1. Know what the Idaho rule says, and what it does not

Idaho's rule for residential assisted living facilities, IDAPA 16.03.22, does not set a standalone transportation standard for residents. Its only direct mention of transportation, in section 156, is in the policy a facility must write if it offers hourly adult care, which must describe transportation services if offered. For most homes, trips fall under other parts of the rule.

Three parts matter most:

- **Outside services (sections 010 and 216).** The rule defines outside services as services provided to a resident by someone who is not a member of facility personnel. The pre-admission assessment must document the need for related outside services, including the service type, name and frequency. Regular specialist visits belong in that record from the start.
- **Resident safety policies (section 154).** Facilities must have written policies on supervision, on staff responsibilities when a resident is absent, and on notifying the right people about changes in a resident's health or mental status.
- **Nursing (section 305).** The nurse is responsible for notifying the physician or authorized provider of medical changes and for telling the administrator about changes that need an amendment to the negotiated service agreement.

If you are unsure how a particular trip fits these sections, ask the Residential Assisted Living Facilities Program, which can be reached at RALF@dhw.idaho.gov.

## 2. Build a trip plan that suits Idaho Falls assisted living residents

A one-page plan for each trip prevents most problems. Settle these points before the day:

1. **Who is escorting.** Decide whether a staff member, a family member or the transport provider goes along, and who is covering the home in the meantime.
2. **How the resident gets there.** Home vehicle, family car, or a transport service. Check with your insurance carrier and attorney about any use of a home-owned vehicle, and about who is permitted to drive it.
3. **What the resident needs on the way.** Mobility equipment, glasses, hearing aids, snacks, a warm coat, and a way to reach the home by phone.
4. **What the clinic needs.** Current medication list, insurance details, the reason for the visit, and any questions from the nurse or family.
5. **The return plan.** Expected time back, and who to call if the visit runs long.

Ask the resident, and where appropriate the family or representative, about preferences. The rule's stated purpose is to provide choice, dignity and independence, and a trip plan is a good place to show it.

## 3. Use the long drive to your advantage

Long distances mean long days, so build the schedule around them. Where possible, book morning appointments in winter, ask the clinic about grouping visits on one day, and allow extra time for the return. Before any highway trip in the cold months, check road conditions on Idaho 511 (511.idaho.gov), the Idaho Transportation Department's travel information service, which has a road conditions layer showing where roads may be slick.

Decide in advance who can cancel or reschedule a trip when conditions turn, and make sure the clinic's phone number is in the staff schedule rather than in one person's memory.

## 4. Check what help is available for Medicaid residents

For residents who are eligible for Idaho Medicaid and have no other means of getting to a covered appointment, Idaho Medicaid offers Non-Emergency Medical Transportation through a contracted manager. The Department of Health and Welfare states that requests should be made at least two business days before the appointment, and that a referral from the doctor may be requested for care outside the resident's community. Eligibility and approval are decided by the program, so check the Department's page before promising a family anything.

## 5. Share the right information, and only that

Sending information to a clinic for a resident's treatment is something HIPAA allows. The U.S. Department of Health and Human Services states that covered providers may share protected health information for treatment purposes without the patient's authorization, as long as reasonable safeguards are used. In practice, that means giving the clinic what it needs for the visit, sending it by a method your home has agreed is secure, and keeping other details out of text messages and casual calls. If you have questions about your own situation, speak to your attorney or privacy officer.

## 6. Write it down afterwards

The visit is not finished when the van returns. Record, in the resident's care record, that the trip took place and the time out and back, along with any new orders, changes to medications, or follow-up dates the clinic gave. Pass those to the nurse the same day, since the nurse is the person who reviews changes in health status. Add the next appointment to your calendar before the paperwork is filed away.

Records entries should carry the date, time, name and title of the person making them, as section 330.03 requires. Records are kept for at least three years under section 330.

## A short checklist

- Is the appointment recorded with the service type, name and frequency?
- Is an escort assigned, and is the home covered while they are away?
- Has the route and weather been checked on Idaho 511?
- Does the resident have what they need for the day?
- Has the nurse received the clinic's instructions after the visit?
- Is the next appointment on the calendar?
