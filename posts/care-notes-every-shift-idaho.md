For owners and administrators of assisted living homes in Boise, Meridian, Nampa and Caldwell, the daily care note is one of the most ordinary documents in the building, and one of the first a surveyor may ask to see. It is also easy to let slip on a busy shift.

This article explains what Idaho's assisted living rule says about care records, and offers some practical habits for keeping notes complete without adding paperwork. Idaho's rule applies statewide, so everything below is the same in Ada County, Canyon County and the rest of the state. If your home is outside Idaho, requirements differ by state, and your own state's licensing agency is the place to check.

## 1. What the Idaho rule says

Residential assisted living facilities in Idaho operate under IDAPA 16.03.22, published by the Idaho Department of Health and Welfare. Section 330.03 covers resident care records. In summary:

- Each resident must have an individual care record.
- Entries must be documented during each shift.
- Each entry is completed by the person providing the care.
- Each entry includes the date, time, name and title of the person making it.

The rule goes on to list the kinds of events that belong in the record. These include treatments, wound care, assistance with medications and any delegated nursing tasks; a resident refusing care or missing prescribed medication; notification of the facility nurse about changes in the resident's physical or mental condition; the results of provider visits and phone calls; notes about outside services such as home health or hospice; and unusual events such as incidents or accidents, along with the facility's response. Read section 330.03 in the official text for the complete list and exact wording, because the rule was revised effective July 1, 2026.

Section 330.02 also states that the facility must comply with the federal HIPAA requirements, which is a reminder that care notes are private health information and belong only with the people who need them.

## 2. Four details that are easy to miss

Most gaps in care notes are small. They tend to fall into four groups:

- **Missing shifts.** A resident has an entry for the day and the evening but nothing overnight. The rule says entries are documented during each shift.
- **Missing time.** The date is there, but not the time.
- **Missing title.** A signature or initials without a role. The rule asks for name and title.
- **Someone else's entry.** The rule describes entries completed by the person providing the care, so notes written later by a colleague from memory are a risk.

## 3. Habits that keep notes complete

Assisted living in Boise and across the Treasure Valley runs on small teams, so the habits that work are the simple ones.

1. **Write during the shift, not after it.** A short note made close to the event is more accurate than a long one reconstructed at the end of the day.
2. **Agree on what always gets a note.** Use the list in section 330.03 to build a one-page guide for your staff: refusals, changes in condition, provider calls, outside services, unusual events.
3. **Use plain, factual language.** Describe what was seen and done. Leave diagnosis and clinical judgment to the nurse.
4. **Make "nurse notified" a standing line.** When something changes, the note should say who told the nurse and when.
5. **Review at handoff.** A quick look at the previous shift's notes at the start of each shift shows both what happened and what is missing.

## 4. A short weekly check for the administrator

Once a week, pick a few resident records and look for the following:

- At least one entry on every shift, for every day of the week.
- Date, time, name and title on every entry.
- A follow-up entry wherever a note mentions a refusal, a fall, a provider call or a change of condition.
- Entries that are legible and, if on paper, in the right resident's file.

Gaps found in a weekly check can be corrected with your staff on the spot. Gaps found by a surveyor cannot. Late entries should be clearly marked as late and never backdated; if you are unsure how your home should handle corrections, ask your nurse or your licensing agency.
